TL;DR

  • Underweight JPY: it ranks worst of the six because the gauge is already moving (broad REER −8.1% over 12 months, after −11.6% in 2026-Q2) while the real policy rate is −0.90.
  • Underweight GBP against USD and CHF: the UK is the only core floater with r − g positive (+0.83pp, 2026-Q2) alongside a primary deficit, and its term premium rose 0.63pp in one quarter to 1.62.
  • Own long duration only in USD and AUD: 10-year marginal yields of 5.13% and 5.14% sit about 2.8pp above breakevens, so nothing is being repressed there yet; own JGBs, CHF and euro long nominals nowhere.
  • Underweight OAT against BTP: France runs a 2.96% primary deficit (2026-Q1) with the spread to Bunds up 9.9bp in a quarter to 81.5bp, while Italy has a 0.70% surplus and trades at 80.1bp.
  • Hold CHF as the currency leg, not its bonds: the best fiscal position of the six (debt 26.5%) comes with a 0.51% marginal yield and a −1.03 real policy rate, so there is no cushion in duration.

Currency. JPY is first because it is the only market where the gauge is already moving. The real policy rate rose from −3.05 to −0.90 over a year, yet the REER kept falling. A currency that falls while policy tightens is the flight tell, though gold in yen is blank, so I cannot confirm it. GBP is second because its arithmetic is the worst but the gauge has not moved yet: REER is +0.13% and GBP/USD is −1.58%. The UK carries 24% linkers (static field, see data issues), which means inflation raises its interest bill and the exit through inflation is partly blocked.

EUR sits above GBP because the bloc identity is benign: r − g −1.10 and primary −1.16 (both 2026-Q1), against the UK's r − g above zero. EUR sits below USD because the euro-area real policy rate is −0.90 against +0.49 for the Fed, and the ECB is the central bank that gets forced to choose with France in the bloc. That is a close call. If the US policy_rate_minus_inflation goes negative while term premium keeps rising, swap them.

USD sits below AUD on debt level (110.8% against 51.1%) and because the US interest bill is about to reprice. AUD's edge is weak: its r_effective is 12 quarters stale and its REER is already +8.2% over 12 months. CHF sits above AUD because Swiss debt is a quarter of US debt and Swiss holders are overwhelmingly domestic (85%, 2024).

Long bonds. Yield against nominal growth shows who is being taxed. JGBs are worst: a 2.63% yield below 3.81% growth, a real policy rate of −0.90, marginal yield up 93bp over the year, and the BoJ holding 46% (2025-Q4) but shrinking its balance sheet by 16 points of GDP. CHF is second worst because the loss comes from the SNB rather than the treasury: 0.51% marginal yield, term premium 0.51, real policy rate −1.03.

EA is third. Yields are 2.9pp above CHF, which is a real cushion, but the OAT carries default risk that Switzerland does not, and the euro-area real policy rate is −0.90. Gilts beat EA because the real policy rate is positive (+0.66) and the market is paying for risk. Gilts are below USD because r − g is +0.83 against −2.03 and breakeven-implied real yield is about 1.9% against 2.8% (marginal_yield minus breakeven_10y, my arithmetic, mixed dates). If the UK breakeven is RPI-based, as it may be, that 1.9% overstates inflation compensation. AUD and USD are close; AUD is first on debt level only, and its inputs are thin.

Where I disagree with the mechanical quadrant. The table's quadrant is sustainable_free for every country it covers, and the latest quarter is blank. It is built from holders_captive and trajectory_unsustainable, which are 3 to 43 quarters stale for the non-euro markets. I do not use it for GBP, JPY, AUD or CHF. For the US, captivity_score jumped 51.9 to 57.3 in 2026-Q1 with no matching move in holder shares, while avg_maturity_years and bill_share went blank that quarter. I read that as a reweighting artefact and do not treat the US as newly captive.

My readings:

  • US: sustainable, drifting towards unsustainable.
  • UK: unsustainable, with holders that are closer to free than captive.
  • Japan: captive; trajectory depends on whether nominal growth stays above trend (see section 3).
  • Euro area: sustainable at bloc level, unsustainable in France.

Breakevens barely moved (US 2.24 to 2.36, UK 3.11 to 3.24) and the US auction_tail is −0.06, so this is real yield and term premium, not an inflation signal. Term premium rose 0.63 in the UK, 0.47 in Australia and 0.38 in the US.

This is stage 2, pricing, without stage 3. Holders are demanding real compensation, which is the opposite of repression. The test is the next move: the Fed's real policy rate is +0.49 against 1.98 a year earlier, with the long end 81bp higher in one quarter. Backtest context: term_premium_proxy warned in 75% of 16 events with a median lead of 12 quarters. It is a regime signal, not a timing one.

US. avg_coupon_gap is 1.60pp (+1.10 over the year): interest cost already committed. My arithmetic gives r_effective near 4.5% within five years if yields hold, since about 60% of the stock reprices. Against g_trend 5.6, r − g goes from −2.03 to about −1.1. With a 2.05% primary deficit the debt ratio then rises about 0.8pp a year, from flat now.

The pipeline's forward_r_5y of 3.77 is from 2025-Q4 and predates the gap widening. Stock-flow residuals of +3.85 (2025-Q4) and +2.70 (2026-Q1) mean debt is rising faster than the identity predicts. Stage-4 tells are not present but are drifting: the Fed's share of the stock rose in 2026-Q1 (14.67 to 14.92) for the first time in six quarters, cb_balance_sheet_growth_minus_g improved from −15.3 to −4.56, and bill_share is up 0.83pp q/q to 22.4%. I read stage 2, against the pipeline's 0.

UK. r − g crossed from −0.38 (2025-Q1) to +0.45 (2025-Q4) and reached +0.83 in 2026-Q2. With a primary balance of −0.08%, any positive r − g lifts the debt ratio, and debt_dynamics is +0.80pp a year. This is stage 1, the trigger, with stage 2 pricing arriving this quarter. The BoE is still shrinking (growth minus g −7.66), so there is no stage 4. forward_r_minus_g_5y of −0.03 (2026-Q2) should be ignored: GB's forward-r RMSE is 2.78, the worst in the backtest.

Japan. Debt/GDP fell 15.4pp over the year to 175.6% (2026-Q1), driven by nominal growth of 3.8–4.7% against an average coupon of about 0.7% (2024-Q4, 7 quarters stale). That is exit (b) in action. It is not locked in. g_trend is 1.82 and the marginal yield is 2.63, so if growth reverts, r − g turns positive by about 0.8pp on a flat primary deficit (−0.21%, 2024-Q4).

The stage tells conflict: a real policy rate of −0.90 and a falling currency (stage 4–5) against a BoJ that is shrinking (stage 4 inverted). I read stage 3–4 at low confidence, against the pipeline's stage 0 (2024-Q4). The gauge cannot be completed without gold in yen.

France. The real policy rate fell from +0.49 to −1.11 in a quarter while the euro-area figure moved from −0.50 to −0.90. That is an outlier I cannot explain from the inputs; treat it as unverified.

Euro area members

These members cannot print. Default is a live branch and captivity is the ECB's willingness. The ecb_backstop_active flag has read 1.0 for every member since 2024-Q4, so it cannot separate "available" from "deployed". Nothing in the data shows deployment: the ECB's share of each stock is falling (DE −3.19pp, FR −2.18, IT −1.82, NL −3.44 over four quarters to 2026-Q1). The captive buyer is retreating.

France is the weak link. Debt is 109.4% with a −2.96% primary deficit and r − g of +0.06 (2026-Q1); with g_nominal at 1.86 (Q2), my arithmetic puts it near +0.1. Stabilising the ratio needs r − g of about −2.7, so the debt ratio rises about 3pp a year (debt_dynamics 3.02). Its g_trend of 3.17 has overstated realised growth for six quarters, so the pipeline's stale forward r − g of −0.57 (2024-Q4) is optimistic.

The market is charging: marginal yield 4.00% (+32bp q/q), spread 81.5bp (+9.9bp q/q, +41bp over five years), term premium 1.75. TARGET2 is −6.5% of GDP (2026-Q2), flat over the year, so there is no flight yet. Foreign holders are 54% (2024-Q4). That is stage 1–2 and the free-holder column, capped only by political tolerance of a backstop. A member-level stage 5 would be a spread above 100bp together with TARGET2 deterioration.

Italy is a primary surplus of +0.70%, r − g +0.11, debt flat at 137.3%, and stabilising r − g of +0.51. My five-year estimate of r (about 3.4%) against g_trend 3.26 gives about +0.2, inside that limit. The spread is 80.1bp (−33.9bp over five years) and TARGET2 is −14.6% (improved from −18.8% in 2024-Q4). The risk is off-budget: residuals averaged +3.3pp over the six quarters from 2024-Q4 to 2026-Q1, and debt rose 2.3pp in five quarters while the identity was flat. BTP at the same spread as OAT is the mispricing.

Germany has the same arithmetic problem on a smaller scale: primary −2.01% (−0.67 over the year), debt dynamics +1.10pp. Debt of 58.7% and TARGET2 of +22.6% (stable) make it the anchor. Netherlands is a non-event: r − g −2.95, spread 10bp.

The ECB's own forward-r record is a caution. Forward r underpredicted realised r by 0.77–0.92pp for DE, FR, IT and NL, while current r beat forward r in 7 of 8 countries tested. If bias means forecast minus realised (the sign convention is not stated), Italy's forward r − g is nearer +0.9 and France's nearer +0.2.

Portfolio implications

JPY. Underweight against AUD, USD and CHF. AUD/JPY carries 2.5pp of yield and REER momentum on both sides. The risk is a carry unwind, which the model does not forecast.

GBP. Short GBP/USD and GBP/CHF as the cleaner pairs. In gilts, prefer the front end, where the real policy rate is positive, over the long end, where the term premium is 1.62 and still rising; avoid 10y and longer. Linkers do not help against a real-yield rise.

EUR. Modest underweight EUR/USD. In rates, hold Bunds and not OATs. Positions: short OAT/Bund and long BTP/OAT. A flatter OAT–BTP spread is the trade, with the ECB share of stock as the risk control.

USD. Own 10y duration at about 2.8% real, but small. The curve is the issue: the real policy rate is 0.49 and the 10y marginal yield 5.13, so the market is pricing the Fed's choice already. Exit rule below.

AUD. Own the bond; this is the least-known market because most inputs are stale.

CHF. Own the currency; skip the duration.

Real assets and gold. The framework favours gold and pricing power in the unsustainable-captive markets, but gold in local terms is blank for every country. I have no data call on gold this quarter, only the framework's. The transfer has barely started in the markets where real yields are positive.

What would change my mind

  1. US stage 4: policy_rate_minus_inflation below 0 (now +0.49) while marginal_yield stays above 5% and cb_holdings_change_4q turns positive (now −0.83, 2026-Q1). Cut USD duration and move USD down the currency ranking.
  2. US trajectory: g_nominal falling below about 4.5 (now 5.66, 2026-Q2) with r_effective rising towards 4.5. r − g turns positive against a 2% primary deficit, and the US moves to unsustainable.
  3. JPY: REER 12-month change turning positive while the real policy rate is still below −0.5. That would mean the market believes the BoJ; move JPY off the bottom. Conversely, a refreshed r_effective at or above g_nominal (3.81) ends the exit (b) story for Japan.
  4. France: spread_to_anchor_bp above 100 (now 81.5) with target2_balance_gdp below −10% (now −6.5%). That is the member-level stage 5 and moves the bond view to outright avoid.
  5. UK: term_premium_proxy back below 1.0 (Q2: 0.99) and r − g back below 0 would take GBP off the second-worst slot. cb_balance_sheet_growth_minus_g turning positive (now −7.66) would signal the BoE choosing the bond market, which is bearish for the currency.

## What changed this quarter and what it means

**Transitions.** None this quarter. The log shows only Italy: a quadrant change and a stage 3 → 6 move in 2024-Q4, then 6 → 0 in 2025-Q3. The stage 6 reading was the primary surplus (+0.4 to +0.6% of GDP) being read as a real fiscal correction. This is not a clean test, because stage inputs are dated 2026-Q2 for US and GB, 2026-Q1 for euro members, and 2025-Q4 for JP, AU, CA and CH. A transition at those dates could not have been detected.

**Largest moves**, 2026-Q2 to 2026-Q3:

- **Global sell-off.** marginal_yield rose in all 11 sovereigns, from +0.16pp (CH) to +0.81pp (US). GB rose 0.64, IT 0.63, CA 0.62, EA 0.61 and JP 0.45. Term premium rose only in the US (+0.38), GB (+0.63), AU (+0.22) and CH (+0.16), and fell in JP, DE, NL and EA. In the US and UK the market is charging for supply (stage 2). In Japan and the euro area the move is a policy-path repricing.
- **Real policy rates fell** in 8 of the 11 sovereigns (US −0.05, EA −0.25, DE −0.31, GB −0.50, FR −0.34, CH −0.36, IT −0.04, CA −0.24). Yields up with real policy rates down means central banks are behind the curve. This is the stage 3 setup, though I have no input showing a pause in hikes.
- **Gold.** Local-currency gold has not been at a record since 2026-Q2 (the flag read 1 through 2026-Q1). The 12-month gain is now +11.4% (AUD), +17.7% (USD), +25.1% (JPY). The USD gain was +62.8% in 2025-Q4. The flight tell (stage 5) is absent for every floater, and this is why I am giving no timing signal.
- **Spread to Bunds.** FR 71.6 → 96.7bp, IT 77.0 → 88.9bp, NL 11.3 → 9.1bp. This is stage 2 for France and Italy. FR is above IT for the first time since 2025-Q4, when the two were 74.7 and 73.9bp.
- **Japan.** The yield rise with a falling currency maps to stage 1–2 in a captive system. The release valve is the yen, not the bond market. Central bank share is flat at 46% and cb_holdings_change_4q is 0.0, but both are carried forward from a manual series and cannot show a stage 4 tell.
- **United States.** The tells that map to stage 4 are present without the earlier stages. The real rate is negative, central bank share is rising and bill share is up. Against that, r − g is −2.03pp (2026-Q2) and the market shows nothing at stage 2. That is the shape of exit (b) beginning.

## Euro area members

Members cannot print. France's marginal yield of 4.44% against g_trend of 3.21% puts new debt 1.23pp above growth, the widest gap in the bloc. Captivity is political: the ECB's willingness to buy skewed.

- **ECB run-off.** The ECB's share of national debt fell 3.4pp in NL, 3.2pp in DE, 2.2pp in FR and 1.8pp in IT (2026-Q1). Run-off is slower in the stressed members, consistent with flexible reinvestment, but all four are falling.
- **Backstop.** ecb_backstop_active has read 1 in every quarter since 2024-Q4 and is carried forward from a manual flag. It is a constant and does not tell us whether the backstop is being used.
- **TARGET2** (2026-Q2): DE +22.6% of GDP, NL +4.1%, IT −14.6% (improved 3.0pp in a year), FR −6.5% (range −3.7 to −6.8). The data predate the Q3 spread move, so no flight is visible or ruled out.

**France** has the lowest captivity score in the table (37.7), the highest foreign share (54%, 2025-Q4), debt of 109.4%, a primary deficit of 2.96% and debt dynamics of +3.0pp a year. avg_coupon_gap is 1.67pp and r_effective is 1.97% (2026-Q1) against a 4.44% marginal yield, so interest cost is committed to rise. The stale forward r of 2.91% implied r − g of −0.27 with marginal yield at 3.56%. Marginal yield is now 88bp higher. With 8.5 years of average maturity and a 2.96% deficit to fund, most of that rise reaches the stock within five years. My own estimate is that forward r − g is now positive. That puts France in unsustainable-free, the cell where the default branch is live and resolution takes a cycle, with the ECB's conditional backstop as the only captive buyer.

**Italy** has debt of 137.3% but a primary surplus of 0.70% and r − g of +0.11pp. The trajectory is flat, the spread is 25bp below its 2025-Q1 level, and the TARGET2 deficit is narrowing. Italy is a real-fiscal-correction story that has not reversed. **Germany** is the anchor, with a primary deficit of 2.01% (widened 0.67pp) and the thinnest term premium in the table (0.31). **Netherlands** shows a 9.1bp spread and r − g of −2.95pp. Its issuance_shortening of +2.81 (+4.57pp in a year) is the only large shortening move in the euro area.

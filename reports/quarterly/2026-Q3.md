## TL;DR

- **Underweight JPY:** it ranks worst of the six because the gauge is already moving (broad REER −8.1% over 12 months, after −11.6% in 2026-Q2) while the real policy rate is −0.90.
- **Underweight GBP against USD and CHF:** the UK is the only core floater with r − g positive (+0.83pp, 2026-Q2) alongside a primary deficit, and its term premium rose 0.63pp in one quarter to 1.62.
- **Own long duration only in USD and AUD:** 10-year marginal yields of 5.13% and 5.14% sit about 2.8pp above breakevens, so nothing is being repressed there yet; own JGBs, CHF and euro long nominals nowhere.
- **Underweight OAT against BTP:** France runs a 2.96% primary deficit (2026-Q1) with the spread to Bunds up 9.9bp in a quarter to 81.5bp, while Italy has a 0.70% surplus and trades at 80.1bp.
- **Hold CHF as the currency leg, not its bonds:** the best fiscal position of the six (debt 26.5%) comes with a 0.51% marginal yield and a −1.03 real policy rate, so there is no cushion in duration.

A currency that falls while policy tightens is the flight tell, though gold in yen is blank, so I cannot confirm it. GBP is second because its arithmetic is the worst but the gauge has not moved yet: REER is +0.13% and GBP/USD is −1.58%. The UK carries 24% linkers (static field, see data issues), which means inflation raises its interest bill and the exit through inflation is partly blocked.

EUR sits above GBP because the bloc identity is benign: r − g −1.10 and primary −1.16 (both 2026-Q1), against the UK's r − g above zero. EUR sits below USD because the euro-area real policy rate is −0.90 against +0.49 for the Fed, and the ECB is the central bank that gets forced to choose with France in the bloc. That is a close call. If the US policy_rate_minus_inflation goes negative while term premium keeps rising, swap them.

USD sits below AUD on debt level (110.8% against 51.1%) and because the US interest bill is about to reprice. AUD's edge is weak: its r_effective is 12 quarters stale and its REER is already +8.2% over 12 months. **Long bonds.** Yield against nominal growth shows who is being taxed. The pipeline's forward_r_5y of 3.77 is from 2025-Q4 and predates the gap widening. Stock-flow residuals of +3.85 (2025-Q4) and +2.70 (2026-Q1) mean debt is rising faster than the identity predicts. With a primary balance of −0.08%, any positive r − g lifts the debt ratio, and debt_dynamics is +0.80pp a year. This is stage 1, the trigger, with stage 2 pricing arriving this quarter. It is not locked in. g_trend is 1.82 and the marginal yield is 2.63, so if growth reverts, r − g turns positive by about 0.8pp on a flat primary deficit (−0.21%, 2024-Q4).

The stage tells conflict: a real policy rate of −0.90 and a falling currency (stage 4–5) against a BoJ that is shrinking (stage 4 inverted). I read stage 3–4 at low confidence, against the pipeline's stage 0 (2024-Q4). The gauge cannot be completed without gold in yen.

**France.** The real policy rate fell from +0.49 to −1.11 in a quarter while the euro-area figure moved from −0.50 to −0.90. That is an outlier I cannot explain from the inputs; treat it as unverified.

## Euro area members

These members cannot print. Default is a live branch and captivity is the ECB's willingness. The `ecb_backstop_active` flag has read 1.0 for every member since 2024-Q4, so it cannot separate "available" from "deployed". Nothing in the data shows deployment: the ECB's share of each stock is falling (DE −3.19pp, FR −2.18, IT −1.82, NL −3.44 over four quarters to 2026-Q1). The captive buyer is retreating.

**France is the weak link.** Debt is 109.4% with a −2.96% primary deficit and r − g of +0.06 (2026-Q1); with g_nominal at 1.86 (Q2), my arithmetic puts it near +0.1. Stabilising the ratio needs r − g of about −2.7, so the debt ratio rises about 3pp a year (debt_dynamics 3.02). Its g_trend of 3.17 has overstated realised growth for six quarters, so the pipeline's stale forward r − g of −0.57 (2024-Q4) is optimistic.

The market is charging: marginal yield 4.00% (+32bp q/q), spread 81.5bp (+9.9bp q/q, +41bp over five years), term premium 1.75. TARGET2 is −6.5% of GDP (2026-Q2), flat over the year, so there is no flight yet. Foreign holders are 54% (2024-Q4). That is stage 1–2 and the free-holder column, capped only by political tolerance of a backstop. A member-level stage 5 would be a spread above 100bp together with TARGET2 deterioration.

**Italy** is a primary surplus of +0.70%, r − g +0.11, debt flat at 137.3%, and stabilising r − g of +0.51. My five-year estimate of r (about 3.4%) against g_trend 3.26 gives about +0.2, inside that limit. The spread is 80.1bp (−33.9bp over five years) and TARGET2 is −14.6% (improved from −18.8% in 2024-Q4). The risk is off-budget: residuals averaged +3.3pp over the six quarters from 2024-Q4 to 2026-Q1, and debt rose 2.3pp in five quarters while the identity was flat. BTP at the same spread as OAT is the mispricing.

**Germany** has the same arithmetic problem on a smaller scale: primary −2.01% (−0.67 over the year), debt dynamics +1.10pp. Debt of 58.7% and TARGET2 of +22.6% (stable) make it the anchor. **Netherlands** is a non-event: r − g −2.95, spread 10bp.

The ECB's own forward-r record is a caution. Forward r underpredicted realised r by 0.77–0.92pp for DE, FR, IT and NL, while current r beat forward r in 7 of 8 countries tested. If bias means forecast minus realised (the sign convention is not stated), Italy's forward r − g is nearer +0.9 and France's nearer +0.2.

## Portfolio implications

**JPY.** Underweight against AUD, USD and CHF. AUD/JPY carries 2.5pp of yield and REER momentum on both sides. The risk is a carry unwind, which the model does not forecast.

**GBP.** Short GBP/USD and GBP/CHF as the cleaner pairs. In gilts, prefer the front end, where the real policy rate is positive, over the long end, where the term premium is 1.62 and still rising; avoid 10y and longer. Linkers do not help against a real-yield rise.

**EUR.** Modest underweight EUR/USD. In rates, hold Bunds and not OATs. Positions: short OAT/Bund and long BTP/OAT. A flatter OAT–BTP spread is the trade, with the ECB share of stock as the risk control.

**USD.** Own 10y duration at about 2.8% real, but small. The curve is the issue: the real policy rate is 0.49 and the 10y marginal yield 5.13, so the market is pricing the Fed's choice already. Exit rule below.

**AUD.** Own the bond; this is the least-known market because most inputs are stale.

**CHF.** Own the currency; skip the duration.

**Real assets and gold.** The framework favours gold and pricing power in the unsustainable-captive markets, but gold in local terms is blank for every country. I have no data call on gold this quarter, only the framework's. The transfer has barely started in the markets where real yields are positive.

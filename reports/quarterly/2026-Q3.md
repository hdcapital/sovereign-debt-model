## TL;DR

- Be short JPY against CHF, and own no JGBs: JPY ranks last on both lists, with a real policy rate of −0.90 and a broad REER down 8.1% over 12 months, though the BoJ is shrinking its balance sheet (−15.5pp of GDP in a year), so keep size modest.
- Hold CHF as the core currency but not CHF duration: the sovereign is clean (debt 26.5% of GDP in 2026-Q1, forward r − g −1.66 at 2025-Q4), but the marginal yield is 0.514%.
- Own duration only in AU and the US, where marginal_yield minus breakeven_10y is 2.77pp in both, and in the US hold linkers over nominals because breakeven_10y is unchanged at 2.36 while the real policy rate fell 1.19pp to −0.09.
- Underweight GBP and gilts: GB is the only core market flagged unsustainable-captive, with r − g at +0.83pp (2026-Q2) and the highest term premium in the table at 1.62, but a +0.66 real policy rate means the BoE has not yet chosen the bond market, so do not short hard.
- In the euro area, avoid Bund duration (term premium 0.026) and prefer BTPs to OATs, because France's spread to Bunds (81.5bp) is now level with Italy's (80.1bp) on a −2.96% primary balance against Italy's +0.70% (both 2026-Q1).

**Currencies.**
- **JPY vs GBP.** The gap is the real policy rate (−0.90 against +0.66) and the gauge (REER −8.1% against +0.1%). Japan's trajectory is the better one: debt fell 15.4pp in a year and r − g was −3.81 at 2025-Q4. That arithmetic is the transfer itself, since nominal growth of 3.8% sits over an effective rate of 0.84%. The mechanical "sustainable_captive" label reads exit (b) as health. The yen has also retraced from its 2026-Q2 low (12m vs USD −11.3% then, −5.9% now), so conviction is lower than the rank suggests.
- **GBP vs USD.** The US has a rest-of-world holder base and a current r − g of −2.03. GB has 32% foreign holders (2025-Q4), no reserve status, r − g of +0.83 and a term premium of 1.62 against 1.08.
- **USD vs EUR.** The euro area has less debt (85.6 against 110.8), a smaller primary deficit (−1.16 against −2.05), a smaller stock-flow residual (+0.67 against +2.70) and a falling term premium (0.44, down from 0.89) where the US premium is rising. Against that, EA real policy is −1.0 and the euro is down 3.3% vs USD over 12 months. I put EUR above USD on trajectory and put USD's reserve status in the margin.
- **EUR vs AUD.** AUD has a positive real policy rate and half the debt. EUR carries member-level default branches and an ECB that is easing in real terms.
- **AUD vs CHF.** AUD's holders are free (45% foreign) and the currency has already risen 8.2% in real terms. CHF costs carry (−0.81 real policy against +0.41) in exchange for no free-holder exposure.

**Bonds.**
- **JGB vs CHF.** JGBs face both price risk and the tax. CHF bonds face neither, but the 0.514% yield is 2.2pp below trend nominal growth.
- **CHF vs EUR.** Euro paper pays 3.43% but with a thin term premium and a retreating ECB.
- **EUR vs GBP.** Gilts pay 1.8pp more with a term premium of 1.62, and the gap to breakeven is the widest of the unsustainable-flag markets.
- **GBP vs USD.** US real yield is about 2.77 against about 1.95 for GB. US r − g is also better, but the US still has 1.6pp of committed interest cost to absorb, so the margin is small.
- **USD vs AUD.** Australia has the lower debt, negative r − g and a positive real policy rate, but its fundamentals are three quarters stale and its breakeven is from 2026-Q2.

**Where I disagree with the mechanical quadrant.**
- **US (sustainable_free, 2026-Q1 vintage).** It is built on a captivity score of 51.9, two quarters old. The table gives no component breakdown, so I cannot see whether reserve status is credited, and the model treats it as captivity. The flag also predates a 1.03pp rise in marginal yield. The identity gives −0.11pp of debt dynamics, but debt rose 3.1pp in the year to 2026-Q1 against 0.4pp from the identity, a residual of +2.70 after +3.85 the quarter before. I treat the US as sustainable on arithmetic only because g_trend is 5.53%.
- **France (sustainable, forward r − g −0.27 at 2025-Q4).** That forecast used a 3.56% marginal yield. The yield is now 4.00%, and 2025 pass-through into forward r was about 0.6, so it is roughly zero today. Backtest bias for France is −0.75, so about +0.5. Realised nominal growth (1.86%) runs well below g_trend (3.21%).
- **Japan.** The stage 0 reading is three quarters stale. Marginal yield has gone from 1.71 to 2.62, above g_trend of 2.02.
- **GB (unsustainable_captive).** The flag is correct but borderline. Primary balance is −0.08% and the 10-year debt projection is flat (88.1 against 86.65 now).

**Forward r is not yet a trusted variable.** In the backtest it beats current r on RMSE only for the US (1.49 against 2.03, n=83). It is worse elsewhere, and its bias is negative everywhere except Greece (EA −1.07, DE −0.79, IT −0.64, JP −0.29). I read negative bias as forward r undershooting realised r, and the sample includes the 2022 inflation shock. Every forward r − g quoted here is therefore probably too low.

## What changed this quarter and what it means

**Transitions.** None were logged for 2026-Q3. The log's only entries are Italy in 2024-Q4 (quadrant to sustainable_free; stage 3 → 6) and Italy in 2025-Q3 (stage 6 → 0). A jump to "Resolution" for a sovereign running a primary surplus of +0.40 reads as a rule artefact, and I give it no weight. The quadrant is blank for every core market in the report quarter. Quadrant and stage values are one to three quarters old, so "no transition" describes stale inputs. The market series are current, and they carry this quarter's information.

**US: stage 2 (pricing), not yet stage 3.**
- marginal_yield went from 4.32 to 5.126 and term_premium_proxy from 0.70 to 1.08, the highest in the eight-quarter history.
- breakeven_10y is flat at 2.36 and auction_tail is −0.06, so the move is real yield and term premium, not inflation compensation, and auctions are clearing without a tail.
- avg_coupon_gap rose from 0.69 to 1.60. That is committed interest cost not yet in r_effective (3.526).
- As a bound, if the whole stock repriced to 5.126% against g_trend of 5.533%, r − g would be −0.41. On 110.8% debt and a −2.05% primary balance that is still about +1.6pp of GDP a year, before the residual (my arithmetic).
- The real policy rate was +1.99 in 2025-Q1 and is −0.09 now. That meets the stage-4 tell at zero, not below it. The Fed's share ticked up from 14.67% to 14.92% at 2026-Q1, the first rise in the history, but it is two quarters old.
- Gold lost its record flag in 2026-Q2. US gold is +17.7% over 12 months, against +62.8% at 2026-Q1. There is no stage-5 tell.
- US interest_to_revenue jumped from 10.5 to 17.2 in a single quarter (2026-Q1) with r_effective unchanged. I read this as a denominator change and exclude it.

**GB: stage 2.**
- avg_coupon_gap closed from −0.33 to +0.04, so the stock rate (5.153%) is now at the marginal yield (5.192%).
- Term premium went from 0.99 to 1.62 and breakeven_10y from 3.11 to 3.24.
- The real policy rate fell from +1.16 to +0.66 but stays positive.
- GBP is −1.3% vs USD over 12 months, with a REER of +0.1%.
- This is the trigger and pricing sequence. The BoE is not subsidising the bond market, so there is no stage 3 or 4.

**Japan: the BoJ is choosing the currency, for now.**
- marginal_yield went from 2.17 to 2.62.
- The real policy rate is up 1.48pp in the year, and the BoJ balance sheet is down 15.5pp of GDP (2026-Q2), with growth minus g at −14.7.
- The yen's 12-month fall eased from −11.3% to −5.9%.
- Stage 3 asks whether the bank holds this stance as yields rise. cb_holdings_change_4q of 0.0 and central_bank_share of 46.0 are carried forward and mean nothing. The balance sheet implies the true share is falling, so the captivity score of 79.8 (2025-Q4) is probably overstated.
- Marginal yield above g_trend by 0.60 puts r − g positive once the stock reprices. At 175.6% debt that is about +1.05pp a year against a +0.48% primary surplus (2025-Q4), but at a 9.4-year maturity it takes a decade.

**Euro area: stage 1 without pricing.**
- Real policy rates fell 0.3–0.6pp in a quarter (EA −1.00, IT −1.09, DE −0.61, NL −0.91, FR −0.11).
- Marginal yields rose 0.2–0.4pp. Term premiums fell by 0.36–0.48 in all four members.
- The market is charging nothing extra for risk and expects the ECB to accommodate. The EA gauge, spread to Bunds, is quiet outside France.

**AU and CH.**
- AU: term premium went from 0.32 to 0.79, and the REER is +8.15% over 12 months.
- CH: real policy went from −0.45 to −0.81, and CHF is −4.6% vs USD. SNB balance-sheet growth is 6.0pp above nominal GDP growth (2026-Q2), which is FX intervention, not fiscal dominance (central_bank_share 0.0).

Gold in local currency is USD gold compounded with FX, so across markets it repeats the FX ranking (AU +11.4%, US +17.7%, GB +19.3%, EA +21.8%, CH +23.4%, JP +25.1%). ## Euro area members

None of the four can print. The ECB can act only at bloc level, with the bloc's inflation shared across members.

- **Backstop status.** ecb_backstop_active is 1 in all eight quarters and is carried forward by hand. A constant cannot signal a change, so I treat it as an assumption.
- **Political captivity.** ECB shares (2026-Q1) are DE 16.1%, FR 10.9%, IT 9.5% and NL 19.9%. Each fell 15–17% over the year, so the run-off is pro rata with no skew toward stressed members. The ECB's willingness (PEPP flexibility, TPI) has not been tested, and nothing in the data shows it tilting.
- **Shared policy rate.** It erodes debt most where it is needed least. The national real policy rate is −1.09 in Italy and −0.11 in France, the member with the largest deficit.
- **France.** It is the member nearest the default branch.
  - Debt is 109.4% (+2.6pp y/y), primary −2.96, debt_dynamics +3.02pp, and avg_coupon_gap 1.67, the largest in the bloc.
  - If the stock repriced fully to 4.00% (my arithmetic), debt would rise about 3.8pp a year at trend growth of 3.21%, and about 5.3pp at realised growth of 1.86%.
  - The OAT spread went from 71.6 to 81.5bp. That is inside its 69–83bp range since 2024-Q4 and not a blow-out, but it is level with Italy for the second quarter in the history (2025-Q4: 74.7 against 73.9).
  - TARGET2 is −6.5% of GDP (2026-Q2), against −5.0% in 2026-Q1. The member-level stage 5 tell (spread blow-out with TARGET2 flight) is absent.
- **Italy.** Primary +0.70, r − g +0.11, debt_dynamics −0.55, debt 137.3% (2026-Q1). The spread is 80.1bp (114bp in 2024-Q4) and TARGET2 has improved to −14.6% (+3.0pp y/y). Italy is the member where captivity has not needed to be invoked.
- **Germany.** Primary −2.01 (−0.67 y/y), debt 58.7%, projection 65.9 (+2.9pp y/y), TARGET2 +22.6%. The Bund term premium of 0.026 prices the anchor as if supply were not rising.
- **Netherlands.** Spread 10.0bp, issuance_shortening +2.81 and bill share 9.2% (2026-Q1). At 40.7% debt that is cash management, not stage 4.

## Portfolio implications

**What is taxed.** Long nominals of unsustainable-captive sovereigns: GB is flagged, Japan is the repression exit in practice, and the euro-area bloc is easing in real terms. The currency is the gauge for floaters and the spread for members.

**Pairs.**
- Long CHF / short JPY is the widest rank gap and my primary pair. The caveat is the BoJ's currency-first stance. Add to it on a yen retrace, and cut if marginal yield passes 3.0 with the real rate improving.
- Long AUD / short JPY has the same direction at lower quality: AUD REER is +8.2% and its holders are free.
- Long EUR / short GBP has the lowest conviction. Trajectory favours EUR (bloc forward r − g −1.48 at 2025-Q4 against GB −0.03 at 2026-Q2). Carry favours GBP (real policy +0.66 against −1.00). Use small size.
- USD sits mid-table. Reserve status makes it the funding-side currency until the central bank share starts rising.

**Curves.**
- US: own TIPS over nominals, since breakeven_10y has not moved while the real policy rate went from +1.99 to −0.09 in six quarters. Prefer the belly to the long end, because term premium is where the repricing is happening. Curve slope is not in the inputs, so this is inference.
- UK: do not own linkers for the repression story, because breakeven_10y is already 3.24. Prefer short gilts while the real policy rate is positive. Long gilts are the taxed asset if the BoE is forced to choose.
- Euro area: no Bund duration. Long BTP / short OAT as a spread. Italy has the lower debt dynamics, and France is where the spread has room to widen.
- Japan: no JGB duration. Yen is the funding side.
- Switzerland: hold CHF cash or short bills, not duration.
- Australia: the one local duration position worth owning, subject to the data caveats below.

**Real assets.** Gold is on the right side of the transfer but off its record (flag 0 since 2026-Q2), so add on weakness, not on breakouts. Equities with pricing power are the other side of the transfer. The inputs contain no equity data, so I give no levels.

## What would change my mind

1. **US to stage 3–4.** term_premium_proxy above 1.3 (a new high) with policy_rate_minus_inflation staying below zero and central_bank_share rising two consecutive quarters above 15.0%. USD drops to rank 1–2 and Treasuries to the bottom three.
2. **Japan upgrade.** marginal_yield above 3.0 with cb_balance_sheet_growth_minus_g still below −10 and policy_rate_minus_inflation above −0.5: the BoJ is choosing the currency, and JPY moves up at least two ranks. The reverse, with growth minus g rising above −5 and gold_local_record back at 1 for JPY, confirms stage 4–5.
3. **France to the crisis cell.** spread_to_anchor_bp above 100 (history high 83.2), or target2_balance_gdp below −10%, or a recomputed forward_r_minus_g_5y above 0 with a primary deficit. That flips France to unsustainable and tests the ECB.
4. **GB stage 4.** policy_rate_minus_inflation below zero (now +0.66) with term premium above 1.6. Alternatively, GB moves to sustainable if r_minus_g turns negative and term premium falls below 1.0.
5. **Australia into the crisis cell.** forward_r_minus_g_5y above 0 (−1.01 at 2025-Q4) or primary_balance_gdp worse than −1.5 (−0.89 now). AUD falls toward rank 2 and the AUD duration view goes.
